9.1 Fares and the fleet

Taxi fares are short-distance and cash-based: UGX 1,000 from Kasubi to Kampala and UGX 2,000 from Bwaise before the crackdown, against UGX 800 to 1,800 on electric buses. E-Bus Xpress ridership was not found.

The number of 14-seaters is not known. Registration snapshots from 2020 conflict: KCCA reported over 15,000 registered and the ministry 14,000 with route charts, while other counts were 12,184, 13,301 route numbers and 7,340 registered out of 13,000 captured. A trade-press summary suggests 15,000 to 25,000 today. Proposed The pilot would count taxis on its corridor with stage surveys. Against the lowest of those estimates, 2,000 vehicles would be about 13%, the proposal's arithmetic; the pilot of 30 vehicles carries 420 seats and cannot affect the market.

Section 10Environmental and social impact

Because Uganda's grid is largely hydropower, each electric 14-seater avoids most of the carbon of a petrol one. Local air and safety benefits are plausible but not yet quantified for minibus taxis.

10.1 Carbon

In the base case one petrol 14-seater emits 14.4 tonnes of CO2 a year, while an electric one charged from the grid accounts for 1.9 tonnes, so 12.5 tonnes are avoided per vehicle per year Indicative. The conservative and favourable cases give 11.6 and 17.2 tonnes. The model uses 2.3 kg of CO2 per litre of petrol, from fleet-software references because primary IPCC, EPA or DEFRA tables were not retrieved, and a grid factor of 0.137 tonnes per megawatt hour, the combined margin in a United Nations standardised baseline.

One caution applies: grid factors differ by vintage: an older Ugandan study reports an operating margin of about 0.614 and a combined margin of about 0.550, and the hydro share is reported as 85% against 68.5% for hydro and bagasse in 2008 to 2012. The older factor would narrow the saving considerably, so the pilot should measure the grid factor at the depot supply.

10.2 Air quality and noise

An electric van has no exhaust. Kampala's PM2.5 level in 2025 was 44.2 micrograms per cubic metre against a guideline of 5 (Section 3), and the national environment authority attributes about 60% of air pollution to transport, a calculation that is unverified. A University of Michigan model of electric boda bodas found CO2 down 36% and nitrogen oxides down 58%, but PM2.5 up 97% because of bagasse and heavy-fuel-oil power plants. No study quantifying the health benefit of electric minibus taxis was found. Noise has not been measured; the pilot would record it.

10.3 Safety, jobs and inclusion

Police recorded 5,383 road deaths in 2025. eTaxi cannot claim a reduction in advance. Proposed It would instead measure incidents per million vehicle kilometres from the first day, use camera and telematics data for driver coaching, and publish the result. Jobs cannot be counted yet because current driver and conductor numbers are not known; the transition plan in Section 6.4 sets the intent. For inclusion, Proposed the pilot would test: a cash and USSD route for riders without smartphones; concession fares for pupils, following the school pass precedent set by E-Bus Xpress; well-lit stages and visible driver identity for women riders; and step and seat design that suits older riders and people with disabilities, written into the vehicle tender.

11.6 Sensitivities and what would break the case

The energy saving is robust: it stays above UGX 58,000 a day in the conservative case. The case is decided by what the saving must pay for. It would break if the electric vehicle premium over a petrol one exceeds the present value in the table above; if lending is dearer than 18.26% or dollar debt meets a weaker shilling; if utilisation falls well below the modelled 112 km a day; if the battery fails before the eight-year life; if the electricity tariff for charging is reclassified upward; or if franchises and stage access are not granted. Energy use is the largest technical unknown, since published projections span 0.39 to 0.93 kWh per kilometre. Each is a pilot measurement.
