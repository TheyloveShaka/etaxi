Section 7Technology and system architecture

One platform connects riders, drivers, vehicles, chargers and regulators. It is designed to work on cheap phones and weak networks, to publish open data, and to protect personal data from the first line of code.

7.1 Scope

In scope: the rider app, the driver app, the vehicle telematics unit and onboard sensors, payment integration, charging management, the fleet operations console, the data platform and the open interfaces that publish schedules and live positions. Out of scope: vehicle manufacture, grid works beyond the depot meter, and any change to government systems. Proposed Every requirement below is a design target, not a measured result.

7.4 Standards

The General Transit Feed Specification (GTFS) and its Realtime extension are community-governed and maintained by MobilityData; a 2013 community effort extended GTFS to informal services with changing routes. A volunteer-built GTFS dataset for Kampala exists, and the MobilityDatabase lists a Kampala feed covering a 14-seater paratransit operator and buses from two operators, with no errors and six warnings in its May 2025 quality report. No real-time feed for Kampala paratransit was found. The Mobility Data Specification is built for micromobility such as e-scooters, so it is not a ready fit for 14-seaters. The Open Charge Point Protocol (OCPP) is proposed as a procurement requirement for chargers so that no single supplier controls charging; the research behind this document retrieved no source on OCPP, so it is named here as a requirement, not described.

7.6 Build versus buy

Proposed Buy what is commodity and build what carries the service. Payments would be integrated through the mobile money operators or a licensed aggregator. Telematics hardware and the camera units would be bought to a specification. The charging-management layer would use OCPP-compliant software, bought or open source. The rider app, driver app, data platform and models would be built, because the local fare, stop and consent rules are the product.

7.7 Delivery approach and environments

Proposed Delivery would run in two-week cycles with four environments: development, test, staging and production. The pilot would run in shadow mode first, with the platform predicting arrivals for existing taxis along a chosen corridor, so the models learn local movement before electric vehicles arrive.

The proposal's view on automation

Boston Consulting Group expects robotaxis to reach emerging markets significantly later than the leading cities, perhaps only after 2040. eTaxi should not plan for driverless operation in Kampala before 2040. The near-term value is driver assistance, safety analytics and a labelled record of African road scenes that others lack.

8.3 Data governance

The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 commenced on 3 May 2019 and its Regulations took effect on 12 March 2021; consent is a central principle and the Personal Data Protection Office sits within the National Information Technology Authority (NITA-U). Controllers must register and renew annually. Location is treated as an identifier, so vehicle position tied to a driver or rider may be personal data, and Regulation 30(2) bars onward transfer of personal data processed abroad to a third country without the data subject's consent. In July 2025 the regulator said prior permission for each cross-border transfer is not required but records of legal basis and safeguards must be kept.

No source tying the Act to CCTV in public vehicles or to taxi trip data was found, and no rule on anonymising trip data. Proposed eTaxi would therefore adopt the following rules and seek the regulator's view on them in the first 90 days.

  • Consent and notice. Location and trip sharing are opt-in in the app; vehicles display camera notices; drivers sign a policy on cameras and driver events.
  • Retention. Camera footage kept for a short fixed period unless tied to an incident; trip records identifiable for the shortest period needed, then aggregated.
  • Anonymisation. Planning exports are aggregated by area and hour with minimum counts so no individual can be singled out.
  • Sharing terms. Three tiers: open schedules and live positions; aggregated data for agencies under a data-sharing agreement; research access under signed terms. Individual-level data is never sold.
  • Accountability. A named data protection officer, registration with the regulator and an impact assessment before the pilot.

8.4 Partnerships

Makerere University's AirQo project combines low-cost sensors, weather data and artificial intelligence, with sensors on buildings, schools and boda bodas. eTaxi could carry sensors on routes and share the readings. Digital Transport for Africa states that at least 105 million people in African cities lack reliable transit information.
