12.1 Gates

Gate 1: pilot to corridor

  • Measured energy use at or below 0.50 kWh per km.
  • Vehicle availability at least 95%.
  • First Ugandan vehicle price, maintenance and charger costs known and in the model.
  • Franchise terms agreed and association agreements signed.
  • Financing term sheets for 300 vehicles.

Gate 2: corridor to network

  • Corridor performance against the model's base case, with variances explained.
  • Unit economics shown with measured costs and the loan terms available.
  • Grid capacity confirmed for the proposed depots.
  • Carbon method and verifier agreed.
  • Funding identified for the next 1,700 vehicles.

All gate thresholds are proposed and would be agreed with the regulator and financiers before the pilot begins.

14.1 Standards

Vehicles would meet the ministry's inspection scope of brakes, suspension, lighting, exhaust emissions and general condition. Chargers would be required to support OCPP, and schedules and live positions would be published as GTFS and GTFS Realtime (Section 7.4).

14.2 Tax position

Reports conflict. A November 2025 report gives 25% import duty on fully built electric vehicles with VAT and income-tax relief aimed at local manufacturing, while the 2024/25 budget was reported to exempt electric vehicles from 18% VAT, stamp duty and income tax. The treatment of a 14-seat electric minibus is not known. A 2026 bill would lower the used-vehicle import limit to 13 years; its passage is unconfirmed and it does not affect new electric vehicles directly. The proposal asks the revenue authority for a written ruling in the first 90 days.

14.3 Data protection registration

The operating company would register as a data controller before processing personal data, appoint a data protection officer and keep records of the legal basis and safeguards for any processing outside Uganda (Section 8.3). Rules on cameras in public vehicles were not found, so the company would ask the regulator for written guidance.
