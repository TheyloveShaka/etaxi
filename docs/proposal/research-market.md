# eTaxi research dossier: business, technology and data

Prepared 13 October 2026 for the eTaxi proposal (privately operated electric 14-seater public taxis, Kampala). This file holds evidence only. Every fact carries a bracketed source URL and the publication date where one was shown; "date not shown" means the page or search result did not show one. Evidence grading is explained under "Conflicts and gaps".

## Key findings

1. Uganda's ERA-approved Q4 2026 (Oct to Dec) commercial electricity tariff averages Shs562.1/kWh (peak Shs666.5, off-peak Shs429.7); the source lists no EV or e-mobility category [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/uedcl-sets-new-power-tariffs-for-final-quarter-5620680; 5 Oct 2026].
2. Kampala pump prices in 2026 moved between about Shs5,049 and Shs5,599 per litre (April to May spot checks) and a reported highest of Shs6,499 petrol and Shs6,599 diesel on 15 June 2026 (see Section 2 for the source set).
3. The only published electric van in the Hiace class with full battery data is the Joylong E6 (12 to 14 seats, 86.1 kWh LFP, claimed about 300 km city range); no African price was found [https://www.busnews.com.au/detail/joylong-e6-1214-seater-full-electric-minibus-785503; 2022].
4. BasiGo's Ma3e seats 16 or 19 (larger than a 14-seat Hiace), claims up to 300 km and a 1.5 h recharge with an 8 year / 400,000 km battery warranty, is assembled in Mombasa, and has over 500 reservations; no price or per-km fee is published [https://www.basi-go.com/ma3e; date not shown] [https://capitalfm.africa/basigo-begins-local-assembly-of-electric-vans-targeting-matatus/; 16 Apr 2026].
5. Global average lithium-ion pack price fell 8% to US$108/kWh in 2025 (LFP packs US$81/kWh) per BloombergNEF [https://about.bnef.com/insights/commodities/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices/; Dec 2025].
6. Uganda charges 25% import duty on fully built EVs, with VAT and income-tax relief aimed at local manufacturing; older reporting says e-vehicles were exempt from VAT, stamp duty and income tax, so the rules conflict and need checking with URA [https://allafrica.com/stories/202511060049.html; Nov 2025] [https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business/uganda-banks-on-tax-exemption-incentive-for-e-mobility-drive--4658852; date not shown].
7. Financing cost is the main economic risk: the average UGX lending rate was 18.26% in April 2026, the Central Bank Rate was held at 9.75% on 13 August 2026, and UGX/USD was about 3,727 on 9 August 2026 [https://eagle.co.ug/2026/04/23/ugandas-shilling-lending-rates-rise-to-18-73-in-february-2026/; 23 Apr 2026] [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/bank-of-uganda-holds-benchmark-rate-at-9-75-as-inflation-edge-prompts-caution-5557436; Aug 2026] [https://currencyfreaks.com/historical/convert/2026-08-09/UGX/USD/5; 9 Aug 2026].
8. Incumbent-operator lessons: Cape Town's MyCiTi got acceptance from all affected taxi licence holders by offering vehicle-operating-company shares or a lump sum, while South Africa's scrapping programme removed about 77,763 taxis against a first-phase target of 135,894 [https://ppp.worldbank.org/node/8507; date not shown] [https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Docs/exe_rq_na/211b4f13-85f2-4c82-ab18-eea62f29d8f0.pdf; date not shown] [https://www.miningweekly.com/print-version/scrap-over-allowance-2019-09-06; 6 Sep 2019].
9. Kampala already has an open GTFS feed for a 14-seater paratransit operator, African road scenes are under-represented in public driving datasets, and Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 treats location as personal data needing a lawful basis [https://mobilitydatabase.org/feeds/gtfs/mdb-1813; May 2025] [https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/17251; 2026] [https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=UG&t=law; date not shown].
10. Uganda's grid is hydro-dominated: UNFCCC standardised baseline PSB0058 gives a combined-margin factor of 0.1370 tCO2/MWh, against about 2.3 kg CO2/L petrol and 2.7 kg CO2/L diesel from fleet-software references; BCG expects robotaxis to reach emerging markets perhaps only after 2040 [https://cdm.unfccc.int/sunsetcms/storage/contents/stored-file-20220811123519440/PSB0058_Final_Recommendation.pdf; 2022] [https://www.husqvarna.com/uk/support/articles/how-does-husqvarna-fleet-services-calculate-my-carbon-footprint-ka-01138/; date not shown] [https://web-assets.bcg.com/pdf-src/prod-live/here-at-last-the-evolution-of-the-robotaxi.pdf; date not shown].

## Section 1: Vehicles

### 1.1 BasiGo Ma3e (Kenya)

- Seats: 16 or 19 passengers. Range: up to 300 km on one charge. Recharge: about 1.5 hours for a full charge. Battery warranty: 8 years or 400,000 km. Battery kWh and price are not stated on the product page. The page describes Pay-As-You-Drive as a "simple mileage-based fee" (BasiGo) with no amount given [https://www.basi-go.com/ma3e; date not shown; page fetched].
- Local assembly: Associated Vehicle Assemblers, Mombasa, using complete knocked down (CKD) kits; first batch of 22 units expected for delivery in April to May (2026); reservation pipeline of over 500 units [https://capitalfm.africa/basigo-begins-local-assembly-of-electric-vans-targeting-matatus/; 16 Apr 2026; page fetched]. The article does not state seats, kWh, charging time, price or lease terms.
- A search-result summary of other coverage names a CATL lithium iron phosphate battery and an NEDC range basis; neither was confirmed on the fetched pages, so treat as unverified [https://khusoko.com/?p=52153; date not shown] [https://tech-ish.com/2026/04/22/basigo-local-assembly-of-electric-vans/; 22 Apr 2026, listed but not read].
- No Ma3e price, deposit or per-km rate was found anywhere.

### 1.2 BasiGo bus programme: pricing signals, scale, order book, funding

- Deposit and fee signals (bus programme, not Ma3e): ImpactAlpha reports operators can acquire the vehicles for around US$7,700 in Kenya and US$8,800 in Rwanda, then pay mileage-based fees [https://impactalpha.com/?p=111969; date not shown].
- Per-km fees reported across BasiGo coverage: KES 20 per km (early programme, covering battery lease, charging and service), KES 40 per km for the larger E9 Kubwa, and a later Business Daily report of Sh65 per km covering energy, maintenance and part of the financing. Attribution within this set of pages was not isolated, so verify with BasiGo [https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/costly-fuel-gives-electric-buses-a-lift-4011852; date not shown] [https://theexchange.africa/electric-buses-kenya-basigo/; date not shown] [https://citizen.digital/article/electric-buses-are-driving-a-silent-revolution-in-nairobi-n314157; date not shown].
- BasiGo says Nairobi operators pay an estimated KES 30 to 50 per km for diesel fuel alone [https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/costly-fuel-gives-electric-buses-a-lift-4011852; date not shown].
- Fleet and backlog: about 130 buses on Kenyan roads and a backlog above 500 units in June 2025; monthly assembly output doubled from 10 to 20 buses [https://techtrendske.co.ke/2025/06/11/basigo-electric-bus-expansion-kenya/; 11 Jun 2025]. An undated report gives over 100 buses, 20,000 daily passengers and nearly 500 pending orders [https://africa.com/top-10-news/basigos-electric-buses-go-national-across-kenya; date not shown].
- 100th BasiGo bus deployed across Kenya and Rwanda, with a stated target of 1,000 electric buses [https://cleantechnica.com/2025/05/27/two-bus-routes-one-in-kenya-and-one-in-rwanda-now-served-by-100-electric-buses; 27 May 2025].
- KL-9 bus (developed with King Long, assembled by Kenya Vehicle Manufacturers) unveiled September 2025, with 20+ units per month planned from 2026 and 1,000 buses by 2027 [https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/1209-48652-kenya-s-basigo-unveils-kl-9-electric-bus-to-support-green-transport; date not shown]. Citizen Digital's headline gives a 320 km range for the King Long design [https://citizen.digital/article/basigo-unveils-320km-range-electric-bus-designed-by-chinas-king-long-n369567; date not shown].
- Funding: about US$42 million, made up of US$24 million Series A equity (Africa50 led) and US$17.5 million debt from British International Investment and the US DFC; use of funds includes assembly kits, charging depots and staff; goal of 1,000 buses in East Africa within three years [https://cnbcafrica.com/media/6363682685112/basigo-secures-42mn-to-accelerate-electric-bus-expansion-across-east-africa-; date not shown] [https://thecondia.com/basigo-secures-41-5m-to-drive-electric-bus-expansion-in-kenya-and-rwanda/; date not shown] [https://mobility-rising.beehiiv.com/p/basigo-raises-42m-for-public-transport; date not shown]. Reported totals differ (US$42 million vs US$41.5 million) and the announcement is dated October 2024 in one search summary and February 2025 in another.
- New Proparco financing for BasiGo reported December 2025, amount not shown in the result [https://allafrica.com/stories/202512100645.html; 10 Dec 2025].

### 1.3 Roam (Kenya)

- Roam Move (bus, not a 14-seater): 170 kWh pack, about 200 km range fully loaded, 80 km/h top speed, fastest charge 1.5 h, price US$135,000, seating up to 51; Roam claims a running cost of KES 20 per km against KES 50 to 60 for diesel (company claim) [https://cleantechnica.com/2023/09/18/roam-introduces-the-roam-move-electric-bus-in-kenya/; 18 Sep 2023]. Focus on Transport describes a 42-seat locally assembled bus [https://focusontransport.co.za/evs-ready-to-roam-in-kenya/; date not shown].
- Roam Rapid (larger): from US$245,000 (KES 30.7 million) per the same search result set [https://cleantechnica.com/2023/09/18/roam-introduces-the-roam-move-electric-bus-in-kenya/; 18 Sep 2023].
- Roam has no 14-seat product in the sources found.

### 1.4 Toyota and Chinese 12 to 16 seat electric vans

- Electric Toyota Hiace: none found in any source. The Joylong E6 is described as a copy of the high-roof Hiace [https://www.autoindustriya.com/auto-industry-news/joylong-e6-is-an-all-electric-hiace-clone-from-china.html; date not shown].
- Joylong E6 (12 to 14 seats): 86.1 kWh liquid-cooled LFP battery; 60 kW, 382 Nm rear motor; importer-claimed range about 300 km city and 280 km highway; 22 kW onboard charger; about five hours on a 20 A circuit; 20 to 80% in 2.3 hours on a 32 A circuit [https://www.busnews.com.au/detail/joylong-e6-1214-seater-full-electric-minibus-785503; 2022] [https://thedriven.io/2022/04/29/new-14-seat-electric-commuter-bus-lands-in-australia/amp; 29 Apr 2022]. An Australian dealer listing gives 73.87 (unit shown as kW, likely kWh), so battery size is reported inconsistently [https://www.australianmining.com.au/detail/joylong-e6-1214-seater-full-electric-minibus-785503; 2022].
- Joylong A-series family: battery 64.8 to 86.1 kWh, motor 50 to 70 kW; the A5 is the 14-seat long-wheelbase high-roof version [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joylong_A-series; date not shown].
- Not found: specs or African prices for Higer, King Long (minibus), Foton, Maxus or Kama electric minibuses. A South African electric coaster (IZI Impala E30) was listed but not read [https://logistafrica.com/en/highlights/izi-launches-impala-e30-africas-first-electric-coaster-with-industry-leading-battery-warranty/; date not shown].
- Energy use evidence for African minibus taxis: a 2023 study of electric minibus taxis in sub-Saharan Africa notes earlier projections of 0.39 kWh/km and 0.93 kWh/km that conflict; seen via a database record only [https://doaj.org/article/577a9283014a4f5fae423f9c85a19af1; Aug 2023].

### 1.5 Retrofit and conversion of existing Hiace vans

- A Kenyan conversion company has electrified Land Cruisers, Land Rovers and Toyota Hiace vans (engine and fuel tank removed, electric motor system fitted, charging stations incl. solar) and is targeting matatus because fixed routes make charging needs predictable [https://www.moveelectric.com/node/51330; date not shown].
- Tembo e-LV kit (Land Cruiser and Hilux, not Hiace): 72 kWh battery, 110 kW motor, about 200 km range, partnership with ETC Mauritius in 2022 (attribution to the same Move Electric page is probable, not confirmed) [https://www.moveelectric.com/node/51330; date not shown].
- General Kenyan figure (not Hiace-specific): conversion kit up to about US$6,000 plus installation up to about US$3,000. Attribution between the two pages is unconfirmed [https://abacus.co.ke/?p=17077; date not shown] [https://businesstoday.co.ke/?p=86167; date not shown].
- No Hiace conversion price, battery size or performance results were found for Uganda, Rwanda or Kenya.

### 1.6 Battery swapping versus depot charging for vans and buses

- No African van or bus study comparing the two was found.
- Global bus studies: a Chalmers life-cycle cost study of BRT systems in 38 cities finds swapping superior in most cases, with stationary charging more competitive where circuit lengths are long and service frequencies high [https://research.chalmers.se/publication/540915/file/540915_Fulltext.pdf; date not shown]. A 2014 paper found swapping preferred at short headways and recharging preferred at long headways [https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/189962?mode=full; 2014].
- Depot charging is the most common e-bus method in India because of low capital cost, low-power chargers and off-peak power [https://cstep.in/drupal/sites/default/files/2022-01/Charging%20technology%20options%20for%20ebus_05Jan22.pdf; Jan 2022]. An MIT study finds a 1:1 vehicle-to-charger ratio gives reliable charging but the highest capital cost [https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/163260; date not shown].
- Swapping is spreading among African motorcycle taxis, driven by commercial operators [https://cleantechnica.com/2026/08/24/gas-stations-are-changing-globally-how-has-it-changed-in-your-town-part-3-africa/; 24 Aug 2026]. Spiro's US$100 million round (Oct 2025) funds swap-network expansion [https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/21/spiro-raises-100m-the-largest-ever-investment-in-africas-e-mobility; 21 Oct 2025].

### 1.7 Battery pack price trend and lifespan

- 2025 BloombergNEF survey (released 9 December 2025): average pack price US$108/kWh, down 8% from 2024; cells US$74/kWh (down 5%); BEV packs US$99/kWh; stationary storage US$70/kWh; LFP packs US$81/kWh versus NMC US$128/kWh; China fell 13% in real terms; further falls expected in 2026 [https://about.bnef.com/insights/commodities/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices/; Dec 2025] [https://www.energy-storage.news/?p=91801; Dec 2025] [https://taiyangnews.info/business/bloombergnef-battery-pack-prices-hit-new-low-in-2025; Dec 2025]. One outlet reports a 1% decrease from a US$97/kWh 2024 figure, which appears to compare inflation-adjusted values.
- Lifespan evidence: BasiGo Ma3e battery warranty 8 years / 400,000 km [https://www.basi-go.com/ma3e; date not shown]. Dakar's electric BRT financing includes a EUR 6.4 million loan for replacing batteries at the end of their eight-year service life [https://hlc.com/en/news/hogan-lovells-advises-proparco-and-eaif-on-the-financing-of-dakar-s-future-electric-brt-bus-network; date not shown].

- South African fleet case: a diesel double-cab at R380,000 versus a R750,000 EV, over five years and 240,000 km, total cost of ownership R1.26 million versus R860,000, which the source calls a 27% saving (the stated totals imply a larger gap; check the original) [https://dealerfloor.co.za/electric-vehicles/electric-vehicles-set-to-become-a-cost-saving-choice-in-africa; date not shown].
- Cape Town and Johannesburg-area bus studies disagree on e-bus economics (see Conflicts) [https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-04-04-e-buses-beat-diesel-in-the-long-run-in-south-africa-study; 4 Apr 2024] [https://newsletter.en.creamermedia.com/article/electric-buses-may-not-be-a-justified-public-transport-endeavour-zutari-2024-07-12; 12 Jul 2024].
- Kampala movement data: floating car data on nine minibus taxis; step lengths averaged 0.83 km (standard deviation 1.9 km) with tails up to 39 km, following a Levy-walk pattern [https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/1388/2904/2114; date not shown; text extracted] [https://nru.uncst.go.ug/handle/123456789/3670; date not shown]. Drivers rent minibuses from owners at a pre-negotiated daily fee (same paper).
- Minibus taxi mode share: taxis 22% of trips in a 2008 travel survey [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/kampala-s-jam-congestion-bad-for-business-report-1804966; date not shown]; minibuses 18.5% of Kampala traffic in the 2010 JICA study [https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12013025_02.pdf; 2010].

## Section 3: Privatisation, franchising and PPP models

### 3.1 Kigali: bus route reform, Tap and Go, e-buses

- BasiGo and AC Mobility (operator of Tap&Go automated fare collection) partnered in July 2023 to bring pay-as-you-drive e-buses to Rwanda, with a goal of 200 e-buses by end-2024; pilot operators were Kigali Bus Service, Royal Express and Volcano; BasiGo integrated its platform with Tap&Go [https://cleantechnica.com/2023/07/28/basigo-partners-with-ac-mobility-tapgo-to-bring-electric-buses-to-rwanda/; 28 Jul 2023] [https://rdb.rw/basigo-partners-with-ac-mobility-tapgo-to-bring-electric-buses-to-rwanda/; date not shown]. USAID granted BasiGo US$1.5 million for Rwanda in November 2023 [https://cleantechnica.com/2023/11/10/basigo-awarded-1-5-million-grant-from-usaid-to-scale-up-clean-public-transport-in-rwanda/; 10 Nov 2023].
- A December 2025 reform moved management from an operator-driven model to a passenger-needs model: Ecofleet Solutions signs service delivery agreements with private operators, paid on service outcomes with penalties for poor service; officials cited outdated fare collection and revenue leakage [https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/36914/National/african-infrastructure-meet-opens/amp; date not shown] [https://allafrica.com/stories/202606290035.html; 29 Jun 2026].
- Reported results (officials and operator, not independently evaluated): government bought 190 buses, taking the operating fleet to about 340; daily ridership rose from about 150,000 to 240,000 (Ecofleet suggests tighter fare-evasion control explains part); public transport share of trips rose from 10% to 20%; about 50 electric buses were about to deploy; government had said all public buses would be electric by end-2026; challenges include driver culture, fuel costs and slow park-and-ride uptake [https://allafrica.com/stories/202605250095.html; 25 May 2026] [https://allafrica.com/stories/202606290083.html; 29 Jun 2026] [https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/36914/National/african-infrastructure-meet-opens/amp; date not shown].
- The 2023 target of 200 BasiGo buses by end-2024 does not appear to have been met in the results; no independent evaluation of Tap&Go integration was found.

### 3.2 Dakar electric BRT

- Operator and owners: Dakar Mobilité (Meridiam and FONSIS). Financing of EUR 135 million: EUR 85.4 million loan split equally between Proparco and EAIF, a EUR 7 million EU grant, about EUR 9 million PIDG TA grant, balance equity from Meridiam and FONSIS; EUR 6.4 million loan for battery replacement after eight years [https://hlc.com/en/news/hogan-lovells-advises-proparco-and-eaif-on-the-financing-of-dakar-s-future-electric-brt-bus-network; date not shown] [https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/senegal/dakar-mobilit%C3%A9-secures-financing-first-ever-100-electric-brt-bus-system-sub-saharan-africa_en; date not shown].

- No measured ridership since launch was found; the 300,000 figure is a projection.

### 3.3 Lagos BRT and LAMATA

- Structure: route concessions to LAGBUS (state-owned asset company) and the First BRT Cooperative Society; LAMATA licenses and manages the corridor, depots and terminals; concessionaires procure and maintain fleets; Primero is the private operator [https://blogs.bard.edu/cepblog/?p=6968; date not shown].
- Fare history: in 2020 Primero withdrew after COVID rules cut capacity from 70 to 20 passengers, then received approval to move fares from N200 to N300 and N300 to N500; 2022 rise of N100 on all routes citing spare-part shortages; 18% rise effective 17 February 2025 [https://thecable.ng/n300-ticket-now-n500-brt-increases-fares-on-all-routes; date not shown] [https://www.icirnigeria.org/lagos-brt-prices-increase-by-n100-effective-july-13/; date not shown] [https://thecable.ng/lagos-to-increase-brt-fares-by-18-february-17; 2025]. Primero's managing director says fares lag costs and the firm is converting to CNG [https://thecable.ng/brt-operator-mulls-fare-increase-old-fare-sustainable; date not shown]. A further fare shock was reported around 30 August 2026 [https://techeconomy.ng/lagosians-shocked-by-new-brt-fares; 30 Aug 2026 header].
- Lesson drawn by the Bard analysis: LAMATA involved the road transport workers union (NURTW) in planning, and the union role tempered displacement compared with Dar es Salaam [https://blogs.bard.edu/cepblog/?p=6968; date not shown]. No ridership figures were found.

### 3.4 South Africa: Taxi Recapitalisation Programme (TRP)

- Target and delivery: first-phase target 135,894 old taxis (adjusted 2007); by September 2018 about 72,653 scrapped at R4.4 billion; a later parliamentary reply gives 77,763 scrapped and over R5 billion paid [https://www.miningweekly.com/print-version/scrap-over-allowance-2019-09-06; 6 Sep 2019] [https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Docs/exe_rq_na/211b4f13-85f2-4c82-ab18-eea62f29d8f0.pdf; date not shown].
- Pace: fewer than 2,000 scrapped in 2019-20; 1,284 in the 2022-23 financial year, with close to 2,000 more identified [https://www.pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/31039/; date not shown] [https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=723026; date not shown].
- Allowance: R50,000 initially; revised to R124,000 in phase two; R129,700 by 2020 [https://www.pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/31039/; date not shown].
- Criticism: allowance judged too low by SANTACO; programme suspended in 2009 when the R400 million allocation ran out; National Taxi Alliance said the initiative failed; operators objected that the recommended Quantum carried fewer passengers than the older Siyaya; eligibility limited to vehicles older than 4 September 2006 with a valid operating licence; illegal panel-van conversions [https://iol.co.za/news/south-africa/2009-01-15-only-8-000-taxis-scrapped/; 15 Jan 2009] [https://www.sowetanlive.co.za/news/2009-05-22-taxi-industry-split-over-transport-plan/; 22 May 2009] [https://mg.co.za/news/2020-08-13-fikile-has-a-daring-plan-for-taxis/; 13 Aug 2020] [https://www.parliament.gov.za/press-releases/remarks-chair-portfolio-committee-transport-mr-donald-selamolela; date not shown].
- The industry carries about 69% of daily public commuters without operating subsidy, per the parliamentary material above.

### 3.5 Cape Town MyCiTi and making taxi owners shareholders

- All taxi associations accepted MyCiTi, and 100% of affected operating-licence holders accepted compensation for surrendering licences and vehicles; owners could take shares in a vehicle operating company (VOC) or a lump sum; Kidrogen is a VOC formed from five taxi associations; VOCs received 12-year operating contracts [https://ppp.worldbank.org/node/8507; date not shown] [https://www.iol.co.za/news/deal-with-taxi-industry-12110306; date not shown].
- Scale: a City statement gives nearly R660 million to 650 operators; a Dunoon figure of nearly R600 million for 662 licences (scopes may differ) [https://groundup.org.za/article/dunoon-long-battle-between-taxis-and-city; date not shown] [https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2019-10-07-dunoon-the-long-battle-between-the-taxi-operators-and-the-city-of-cape-town/; 7 Oct 2019].
- What went wrong: in Dunoon, removal of minibus services led to illegal operators and conflict [https://groundup.org.za/article/dunoon-long-battle-between-taxis-and-city; date not shown].

### 3.6 Dar es Salaam DART

- DART's first-phase operator UDA-RT was a joint venture between UDA and daladala operators; an MoU with Simon Group had daladala owners selling vehicles to UDA and rolling proceeds into UDA shares for a 20% stake (delivery unconfirmed); ownership was contested between the government (49%) and Simon Group (51%); daladala owners received cash compensation at launch [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam_Rapid_Transit; date not shown] [https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/uda-eyes-over-sh200bn-in-listing-at-dse-2514682; date not shown].
- Later phases: Sh102 billion approved for compensation on Phases IV and V; a Sh96.57 billion compensation dispute is delaying Phase V and risks higher costs; a Sh5.7 billion release for Phase III payouts was reported [https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/delayed-brt-compensation-risks-raising-costs-of-key-dar-road-plan-5589978; date not shown] [https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/news/Tanzania--government-releases-Sh5-7-billion-for-3rd-phase-BRT/1840340-5522794-format-xhtml-n86r5hz/index.html; date not shown].

### 3.7 Nairobi SACCOs and electric operators

- A Nairobi sacco reports improved returns, lower costs and passenger appeal after switching to electric buses; no percentage given [https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/corporate/shipping-logistics/operators-say-electric-buses-make-more-profit-cut-costs-5347284; date not shown]. BasiGo's model keeps private owner-operators and adds subscription charging and service (Section 1.2).

### 3.8 Incumbents absorbed rather than displaced

- Cape Town (VOC shares), Lagos (union in planning, private concessionaires), Kigali (operators retained under outcome-based service agreements) and BasiGo (financing of existing sacco and private owners) are the examples found. Dar es Salaam is the partial and contested case.
- Lessons (analyst synthesis of the sources above, not a quoted finding): combine cash and equity offers; settle ownership before tender; keep a feeder role for incumbents on secondary roads; budget compensation and track payment delays; expect informal re-entry where routes are removed; fare adjustment mechanisms are a recurring flashpoint (Lagos); FX and debt risk is carried by lenders through concessional blends in Dakar.
- FX-risk evidence specific to bus operators: none found beyond Dakar's concessional loans and Lagos fare pressure.

### 4.2 Evidence on African road data and AV/ADAS

An earlier ETASR paper benchmarks YOLO models on an African road obstacle set [https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/9135; date not shown].

### 4.6 Privacy and legal constraints (Uganda)

- The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 commenced 3 May 2019; the Regulations took effect 12 March 2021; consent is a central principle; the Personal Data Protection Office sits within NITA-U [https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=UG&t=law; date not shown] [https://www.dataguidance.com/news/uganda-data-protection-and-privacy-regulations-passed; date not shown].

South Africa's POPIA is flagged as a constraint on telematics data resale there [https://www.logisticsbusinessafrica.co.za/when-data-protection-meets-fleet-performance/; date not shown].

## Section 5: Climate and finance

### 5.1 Emission factors

- Petrol 2.3 kg CO2 per litre, diesel 2.7 kg CO2 per litre (fleet-software references) [https://www.husqvarna.com/uk/support/articles/how-does-husqvarna-fleet-services-calculate-my-carbon-footprint-ka-01138/; date not shown] [https://knowledge.powerfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/22269747018258-How-CO2-emission-value-is-calculated; date not shown]. Other references: about 2.31 (petrol) and 2.67 (diesel) [https://sensorautomotriz.com/preguntas-frecuentes/cuanto-co2-emite-un-coche-de-gasolina; date not shown]; 2.4 for petrol in a Quebec government document [https://archives.bape.gouv.qc.ca/sections/mandats/traverse_oka/documents/DQ5-1.pdf; date not shown]. IPCC, EPA or DEFRA primary tables were not retrieved.

### 5.2 Uganda grid

- Hydropower and bagasse made up on average 68.5% of generation in 2008 to 2012 (UNFCCC assessment) [https://cdm.unfccc.int/sunsetcms/storage/contents/stored-file-20150727164902965/Assessment_report_Uganda%20GEF_final_20140922.pdf; 2014]. An I-REC country assessment says hydro supplies 85% of grid power [https://www.trackingstandard.org/wp-content/uploads/I-REC-Country-Assessment-Uganda_v1.pdf; date not shown]. A Bujagali project page claims 90% renewable (promotional) [https://suelzle-gruppe.de/en/bujagali-hydropower-project-in-uganda-en/; date not shown].
- Grid emission factors: UNFCCC standardised baseline PSB0058 lists operating margin 0.2740 and combined margin 0.1370 tCO2/MWh (0.2055 for wind and solar); an older Uganda study reports operating margin about 0.614 and combined margin about 0.550 [https://cdm.unfccc.int/sunsetcms/storage/contents/stored-file-20220811123519440/PSB0058_Final_Recommendation.pdf; 2022] [https://cdm.unfccc.int/sunsetcms/storage/contents/stored-file-20140407115440958/Uganda%20GEF%20Report%2003Dec2013%20TR.pdf; Dec 2013]. The IEA Uganda 2023 report is cited by Climatiq but no value was read [https://iea.org/reports/uganda-2023/executive-summary; 2023].

### 5.3 Local air pollution

- Kampala annual PM2.5 exceeds WHO guidelines by at least 8 times; about 19% of non-accidental premature adult deaths in 2018 to 2021 were attributable to long-term PM2.5; diesel minibus taxis averaged 25 years old with NOx more than nine times the Euro 4 limit, and at least 16% of those over 15 years emitted visible black smoke [https://theicct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/ID-400-%E2%80%93-Kampala-remote-sensing_TRUE_final.pdf; Jun 2025] [https://www.unep.org/technical-highlight/kampala-faces-severe-vehicle-pollution-crisis-reveals-first-study-its-kind; date not shown].
- NEMA attributes about 60% of air pollution to transport (calculation unverified) [https://allafrica.com/stories/202609080064.html; 8 Sep 2026]. A 2026 low-cost-sensor study found direct traffic emissions at 18 to 35% of PM2.5 depending on road proximity and resuspended dust above 50% [https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-14688.html; 14 Mar 2026].
- University of Michigan model of electric boda bodas: CO2 down 36%, CO 90%, NOx 58%, hydrocarbons 99%, but PM2.5 emissions up 97% because of bagasse and heavy-fuel-oil plants, with a health argument from moving emissions away from people [https://news.umich.edu/electrifying-motorcycle-taxis-in-kampala-uganda-shows-air-pollution-benefits; Feb 2022]. No study quantifying health benefits of electrifying minibus taxis was found.
- Early enforcement: PM2.5 averaged 36.8 ug/m3 before a taxi crackdown week and 32.4 ug/m3 after (one week only) [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/kampala-pollution-drops-amid-dmc-taxi-crackdown-5580444; 2026]. Government has deployed 21 electric buses in Kira Municipality and is adopting Euro 4 standards (same source set).

### 5.4 Carbon credit methodologies and prices

- Verra's VMR0014 is a minor revision of CDM methodology AMS-III.C (introduction of new electric and hybrid vehicles displacing fossil vehicles) to include electric mobile machinery [https://verra.org/?p=65566; date not shown] [https://verra.org/?p=67419; date not shown]. Verra's fleet-efficiency methodology (trucks, buses, taxis) is based on CDM AMS-III.BC and covers efficiency, not electrification.
- BasiGo secured Gold Standard certification for its e-bus project (first of its kind in Africa); first credits expected to be sold to Climate Impact Partners; the search summary dated certification to April 2026 [https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/1304-54628-kenyan-e-mobility-firm-basigo-certified-to-sell-carbon-credits-from-buses; date not shown].
- Prices: no e-bus credit price found. General voluntary market references: nature-based removals US$7 to 24 per tonne; technology removals EUR 150 to 500 per tonne (vendor blog); removals often priced more than 300% above avoidance credits (one source); Thailand domestic THB 50 to 200 per tonne [https://carboncredits.com/voluntary-carbon-market-in-2026-top-forecasts-and-what-they-mean-for-investors/; 2026] [https://www.regreener.earth/blog/carbon-credit-prices-today-trends-and-forecasts-for-2026; 2026] [https://carbon-pulse.com/525025/; date not shown]. Market size estimates for 2026 range from about US$1.7 billion to US$23.8 billion across sources. ## Section 6: Projections

### 6.1 Africa and East Africa EV and e-bus outlook

- A 2026 ETH Zurich study of 52 African countries to 2040 finds battery-electric vehicles charged by solar off-grid systems cost-effective well before 2040 in most countries and segments; electric cars and two-wheelers competitive from as early as 2030; small cars the hardest segment; financing is the main barrier; results exclude taxes, duties and subsidies. EVs were about 1% of new car sales in Africa in 2025 [https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/e9beae22-2d8b-4036-bd67-e28560c12e4e; 2026] [https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/11/1132714/evs-africa-cost/amp/; 11 Feb 2026].
- An earlier forecast (Shell Foundation-linked, pre-2025) projected EV sales in Ethiopia, Kenya, Nigeria, Rwanda and Uganda of 340,000 to 820,000 in 2025, rising to about 3.8 to 4.9 million by 2040, with 4 to 8% of the vehicle parc electric by 2030 and 25 to 35% by 2040; compare with the observed 1% share [https://www.mckinsey.com/za/our-insights/power-to-move-accelerating-the-electric-transport-transition-in-sub-saharan-africa; date not shown] [https://energyforgrowth.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/The-Drivers-of-Africas-Electric-Vehicle-Future-Might-Be-Different-Than-You-Think.docx-3.pdf; Oct 2022 upload].
- Kenya buses: 6,370 buses sold from 2018 to 2022 (average 1,274 a year); a CleanTechnica projection built on BasiGo's 1,000-bus target implies market shares of 4.4% (2025), about 6.7% (2026) and 15.6% (2027), an assumption-based projection, not reported sales [https://cleantechnica.com/2025/05/27/two-bus-routes-one-in-kenya-and-one-in-rwanda-now-served-by-100-electric-buses; 27 May 2025]. Electric motorcycles reached 7.1% of Kenyan sales in 2024; Ethiopia electric share of new registrations around 60% in 2024 (single source) [https://climateos.beehiiv.com/p/why-cars-are-losing-the-electrification-race-to-two-and-three-wheelers-in-east-africa-1f60; date not shown].
- Vans: no Africa van cost-parity study found. Context: vans in sub-Saharan Africa are often bought used and owned by individuals who hire them out (nearly 50% of vans in Kenya per one source), so financing and income constraints dominate [https://www.mckinsey.com/za/our-insights/power-to-move-accelerating-the-electric-transport-transition-in-sub-saharan-africa; date not shown] (attribution probable).

### 6.2 Urban population and travel demand (Kampala)

- Kampala from about 4 million to 7 million at a 5.32% compound rate (UN World Urbanization Prospects via World Population Review, no year given in the article) [https://monitor.co.ug/uganda/business/markets/kampala-s-population-to-grow-to-seven-million-people--4759018; date not shown]. Greater Kampala (GKMA) growth of 4.24% a year to 2030, from 5.2 million up by 3.3 million [https://monitor.co.ug/uganda/business/markets/greater-kampala-needs-62-000-housing-units-annually-to-contain-rising-population-says-report--4726114; date not shown] (attribution probable). ### 6.3 Global electric bus adoption

- Electric bus sales rose about 12% to roughly 70,000 in 2025, about 60% in China; US sales fell 40% (IEA, via secondary coverage) [https://kfgo.com/2026/08/19/china-drove-global-electric-truck-bus-sales-above-half-a-million-in-2025/; 19 Aug 2026] [https://electrek.co/2026/05/19/iea-global-ev-sales-headed-for-another-record-year-despite-the-slowdown/; 19 May 2026]. The IEA's 2025 outlook put 2024 sales at more than 70,000 and China's share below 70% in 2024, down from about 99% in 2017 [https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/trends-in-heavy-duty-electric-vehicles; 2025].
- BloombergNEF's EV Outlook 2026: more than 20 countries had electric buses above 50% of city bus sales in 2025, and electric and fuel-cell buses are forecast to reach about 60% of global bus sales by 2030 [https://www.sustainable-bus.com/news/bloomberg-nef-evo-2026-report-electric-buses/; 2026]. Zero-emission buses reached 60% of EU Class I sales in 2025 (same result set).

### 6.4 Autonomous vehicle and robotaxi outlook

- Waymo: about 500,000 paid rides per week across 10 US cities in March 2026 (earlier reports about 400,000 across six cities); target above 1 million paid weekly rides in the US by end-2026; fleet about 3,500 robotaxis; fully driverless operation activated in Las Vegas in July 2026 with Denver, San Diego and Tampa next; plans for new commercial services in 20 cities in 2026 and later London and Tokyo [https://noqta.tn/en/news/waymo-500k-weekly-rides-robotaxi-10-cities-2026; Mar 2026] [https://amp.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/02/12/335668.htm; 12 Feb 2026] [https://electrek.co/2026/07/08/waymo-driverless-las-vegas-four-new-cities/; 8 Jul 2026]. Lux Research puts operating cost as low as US$0.29 per mile; Tesla's Musk claimed 20 cents per mile for the Cybercab (a company claim) (attribution to these pages probable) [https://www.eetasia.com/making-the-business-case-for-robotaxis/; date not shown] [https://insideevs.com/news/736936/tesla-robotaxi-cybercab-price/; date not shown].
