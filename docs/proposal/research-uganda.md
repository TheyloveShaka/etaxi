# Research dossier: public transport in Uganda, Kampala and the GKMA

Prepared 13 October 2026 as evidence for an eTaxi proposal (privately operated electric 14-seater public taxis). This file is evidence only. It contains no proposal text.

## Key findings

1. The Ministry of Works and Transport and the Uganda Police Traffic Directorate launched "Operation Ganyegenya", a 100-day crackdown on commuter taxis in dangerous mechanical condition (DMC) across Greater Kampala and major highways, which started Monday 24 August 2026 [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/only-69-impounded-taxis-cleared-to-return-to-kampala-roads-says-works-ministry-5618090, 3 Oct 2026].
2. By 2 October 2026 the ministry said 477 taxis had been seized, only 69 had been repaired and passed an automated fitness test, 408 were still grounded, and number plates of all impounded vehicles had been confiscated [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/only-69-impounded-taxis-cleared-to-return-to-kampala-roads-says-works-ministry-5618090, 3 Oct 2026].
3. The test is condition, not age: no taxi age cap or 14-seater ban was found in any 2026 source; the targeted vehicles are those "beyond repair", and well-kept newer vans are not targeted [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026].
4. The first days caused a supply shock: a taxi leader estimated about 75 percent of taxis were off the road by day two, some fares doubled (Kasubi to Kampala from Shs1,000 to Shs2,000), and boda boda riders raised fares by up to Shs3,000 [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-soften-dmc-taxi-crackdown-after-chaos-5571686, 26 Aug 2026; https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/commuters-stranded-in-travel-chaos-5570062, 25 Aug 2026].
5. Operators (UTOF) blame the ageing fleet on the 2018 ban on importing vehicles 15 years or older, on cost of newer vans, and on being excluded from reforms; a bill tabled 1 April 2026 would cut the import limit to 13 years, and its passage is unconfirmed [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-bans-vehicles-in-dangerous-mechanical-condition-5396010, 19 Mar 2026; https://bills.parliament.ug/attachments/FPED3-26-Report%20on%20the%20Traffic%20and%20Road%20Safety%20(Amendment)%20Bill,%202026%20%20(Including%20Minority%20Report).pdf, Apr 2026].
6. Government's visible replacement answer is electric buses, not electric taxis: Kiira Motors' Kayoola buses run by E-Bus Xpress (8 buses from May 2026, about 30 by end August, 100 targeted by December 2026 and at least 300 by December 2027) at Shs800 to Shs1,800 a trip, cashless only [https://www.pulse.ug/story/e-bus-xpress-free-rides-kampala-routes-fares-2026083114302199453, 31 Aug 2026].
7. Taxi owners are themselves moving toward electric: the Union Transport Alliance said on 13 August 2026 that first electric taxis could run before end-2026, bought on instalments, if battery-swap stations are built first [https://www.pulse.ug/story/kampala-electric-taxis-2026-2026081312485458856, 13 Aug 2026].
8. Kampala has no formal mass-transit backbone; a Ministry of Works document gives a mode split of about 48 percent walking, 33 percent taxi, 10 percent boda boda, 9 percent other including private car (undated), and an IGC study put congestion cost at USD 1.5 million a day for the GKMA [https://www.ric.works.go.ug/rc/files/7.6_Development_Public_Transport_Policy_Kampala.docx, date not shown; https://www.theigc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Baertsch-2020-policy-brief-19148.pdf, 2020].
9. Roads are lethal and the air is poor: police recorded 26,044 crashes and 5,383 deaths in 2025, and Kampala's 2025 PM2.5 annual average was 44.2 micrograms per cubic metre against a WHO guideline of 5 [https://eagle.co.ug/2026/03/30/road-carnage-over-4600-people-killed-in-26044-crashes-in-2025/, 30 Mar 2026; https://ugbusiness.com/2026/03/energy-oil-gas/environment/uganda-ranks-among-worlds-most-polluted-countries-as-air-quality-worsens, Mar 2026].
10. Past private-operator rescues of Kampala transport failed (Pioneer Easy Bus from 2012, Tondeka Metro planned for 2020), and PPP Act 2015 requires cabinet and parliamentary approval for major PPPs; no ERA tariff specific to EV charging, no NDP IV transport targets, and no taxi fleet-renewal financing scheme were found [https://songhaiadvisory.com/blog/2022/5/6/peak-troughs-and-people-surround-ugandan-capitals-bus-transit-plans, 6 May 2022].

## 1. The crackdown on old taxis in Kampala (Priority 1)

### 1.1 Who ordered it, what it is, where it applies

- The Ministry of Works and Transport launched a 100-day joint enforcement operation named Operation Ganyegenya; Minister Fred Byamukama announced it at a Kampala press briefing and the directive was issued to the Uganda Police Force [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026].
- The operation was jointly launched on 24 August 2026 by the ministry and the Uganda Police Traffic Department, and targets commuter taxis (public service vehicles) in dangerous mechanical condition on major highways and in the GKMA [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/only-69-impounded-taxis-cleared-to-return-to-kampala-roads-says-works-ministry-5618090, 3 Oct 2026].
- The 20 August article itself did not state a start date [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026]; a following Monitor piece, headlined as starting "today", reported the minister's statement that from Monday 24 August no DMC taxis would be allowed in the GKMA [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/operation-against-dmc-taxis-starts-today-5568622, date not shown].
- Minister's title varies by article: "Minister for Transport" [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026], "Works State Minister" [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-bans-vehicles-in-dangerous-mechanical-condition-5396010, 19 Mar 2026], "Minister of Works and Transport" [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-soften-dmc-taxi-crackdown-after-chaos-5571686, 26 Aug 2026]. CONFLICT on title only; the name is consistent.
- Area covered: Kampala, Wakiso and Mukono districts, with checkpoints on Gayaza Road, Jinja Road and Hoima Road [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-impound-178-vehicles-on-day-one-of-crackdown-on-dmc-5569952, date not shown].
- Target description: commuter taxis "beyond repair", known locally as gaganyaganya, kinyegenya or ganyegenya; newer well-kept vans (called "drones") are not targeted if they meet basic mechanical standards, and owners of vehicles in good condition may be guided to repair rather than face impoundment [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026].
- Faults the minister cited: torn seats, super-glue repairs, a door held shut with rope and a steering wheel tied with rope; he also said dilapidated vehicles are used by criminals to hijack and rob passengers [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026].
- Police-listed faults: worn tyres, defunct brakes and headlights, broken and rusty bodies; some vehicles were judged by sight; drivers were not arrested but sent home [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-impound-178-vehicles-on-day-one-of-crackdown-on-dmc-5569952, date not shown].
- Minister's stance: the government will not back down under political threats or strike warnings and has no point of negotiation; the same article notes past taxi-operator strike threats and threats to withhold votes from the ruling party, and says past crackdowns were often suspended after such pressure [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026].

### 1.3 How enforcement changed in the first days

- On day two Traffic Police eased the operation after a meeting with the Minister of Kampala Metropolitan Affairs and traffic commanders: only taxis in notoriously bad shape would be impounded, the day was set aside for repairs, and officers promised to impound at least 10 taxis per route as a sample so operators could see what counts as DMC [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-soften-dmc-taxi-crackdown-after-chaos-5571686, 26 Aug 2026].
- UTOF chairman Rashid Ssekindi said day one was well received with many operators taking vehicles for repair, that drivers on day two did not know which vehicle counted as a DMC, and that operators and officials were to meet to review the operation, with police to present findings and a compliance timeline [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-soften-dmc-taxi-crackdown-after-chaos-5571686, 26 Aug 2026].
- Drivers also reported police checking driving permits and third-party insurance, not only DMC status (taxi driver Musa Gasak, Total-Kibuli stage) [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/commuters-stranded-in-travel-chaos-5570062, 25 Aug 2026].
- Garages: mechanic Tony Kisibo said prices were rising with demand; mechanics said most taxis needed work on dashboards, interiors, paint, wiring and tyres [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-soften-dmc-taxi-crackdown-after-chaos-5571686, 26 Aug 2026].

### 1.4 The run-up in 2026

- 18 to 19 March 2026: Works State Minister Fred Byamukama issued a 20-day directive on old, unroadworthy taxis, with removals to begin 8 April 2026; owners were to bring vehicles for inspection at designated centres, and the minister said most old taxis failed; hundreds of unfit taxis were expected to be removed; no fines were stated [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-bans-vehicles-in-dangerous-mechanical-condition-5396010, 19 Mar 2026].
- Ssekindi backed the safety aim but said he learned of the directive from the media and criticised the lack of consultation; Moses Balagadde of the Busabala-Kategula stage said stakeholders and drivers should be sensitised through seminars [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-bans-vehicles-in-dangerous-mechanical-condition-5396010, 19 Mar 2026].
- A Monitor editorial headline from the period urged sensitising taxi owners before the ban on DMCs (headline only read) [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/oped/editorial/sensitise-taxi-owners-before-ban-on-dmcs-5397210, date not shown].
- A 21 April headline listed in a later Monitor article says operators opposed plans to remove old taxis and demanded more time; the article text itself was not read [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/govt-launches-100-day-crackdown-on-unroadworthy-taxis-in-greater-kampala-5564908, 20 Aug 2026]. What actually happened on or after 8 April is not documented in the sources found (see Gaps).
- 26 February 2026 (photo-caption date): KCCA was reported set to unveil a transport plan banning taxis from picking up and dropping off outside designated stops and from operating outside designated parks; spokesperson Daniel Nuwabine said all Entebbe Road taxis must go into Usafi Park and enforcement was under way. The article was partly paywalled and the final rule and effective date were not confirmed [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/-kcca-bans-taxi-street-drops-pick-ups-in-new-regulations-5373638, about 26 Feb 2026].
- 2022 minister's order moving Entebbe Road taxis to Usafi and Kisenyi parks was reported as not obeyed at the first deadline (undated KFM item, shown in a search summary) [https://kfm.co.ug/taxi-operators-defy-ministers-directive-of-moving-to-usafi-kisenyi-parks, date not shown].

### 1.5 Reactions

Taxi operators and their associations
- UTOF told members whose vehicles were in dangerous condition to park them before impoundment; many operators did, which removed supply [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/commuters-stranded-in-travel-chaos-5570062, 25 Aug 2026].
- UTOF vice chairperson (administration) Mustapha Mayambala acknowledged many taxis are in poor condition, blamed high fuel prices, bad roads and high taxes, and urged owners to overhaul engines rather than only repair seats [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/commuters-stranded-in-travel-chaos-5570062, 25 Aug 2026].
- Ssekindi blamed the ageing fleet on the ban on importing commuter taxis older than 15 years from manufacture, said affordable replacements are unavailable, and said few people will invest in 14-seaters because they are expensive and returns are low [search-summary of https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/operation-against-dmc-taxis-starts-today-5568622, date not shown].
- In March 2023 Ssekindi said a used Toyota Hiace that may legally be imported costs Shs80m to Shs90m, a new Hiace carries at least Shs28m in taxes, and he asked government to cut taxes on passenger vans or allow cheaper older models [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/why-kampala-city-s-taxis-are-falling-apart-4152234, 9 Mar 2023].
- On 5 May 2026 Mayambala said KCCA had not consulted operators before launching the electric bus system, argued environmental concerns should not exclude operators, and said UTOF was not in conflict with KCCA. His words: "We need to be part of the conversation." [https://allafrica.com/stories/202605060099.html, 5 May 2026, originally Nile Post].
- A Monitor headline records that the electric-bus launch irked taxi operators; a search summary attributes to the UTOF chairman the complaint that tax-exempt, government-funded buses create an unlevel playing field (summary only) [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/launch-of-electric-buses-irks-taxi-operators-5448494, date not shown].
- Boda boda and taxi unions announced an alliance in September 2025 (headline only read) [https://allafrica.com/stories/202509110389.html, Sep 2025 per URL code, exact date not shown].
- Operators attribute the rise in DMC taxis to high operating costs, rising vehicle prices, traffic congestion and competition from motorcycles and passenger trains [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/police-impound-178-vehicles-on-day-one-of-crackdown-on-dmc-5569952, date not shown].

Strikes
- No 2026 taxi strike against the DMC crackdown was found in the search results; the KFM strike items returned were undated and look older [https://kfm.co.ug/over-40-striking-city-transporters-arrested, date not shown].
- In 2025 taxi drivers announced a strike over automated Express Penalty Scheme fines, and the ministry suspended the scheme from 12 June to 12 July 2025, later extending the suspension [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/taxi-drivers-announce-strike-over-auto-eps-5078422, 2025, exact date not shown; https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/uganda-extends-suspension-of-auto-eps-amid-ongoing-review-5116548, 2025, exact date not shown].

Commentary

### 1.7 The rules in force and the legal levers

Vehicle age
- Import limit: the Traffic and Road Safety (Amendment) Act 2018 banned importing vehicles 15 years old or older, counted from manufacture; the Uganda Revenue Authority customs commissioner announced it for 1 October 2018; heavy specialised vehicles such as breakdown lorries and fire engines were exempt [https://www.parliament.go.ug/news/1514/importation-old-cars-banned, date not shown; summary of https://kfm.co.ug/amendment-bill-that-effects-ban-on-the-importation-of-old-cars-passed, date not shown].
- In 2018 an MP said over 5,500 vehicles were stuck at URA offices and the finance minister said taxes could not be waived without amending the law [https://www.kfm.co.ug/news/mp-nsereko-over-5500-vehicles-affected-by-ban-on-importation-of-old-cars.html, 2018, exact date not shown].
- 2026 proposal: the Traffic and Road Safety (Amendment) Bill 2026, first read 1 April 2026, would lower the maximum import age from 15 to 13 years with a 50 percent environmental levy above 13 years, as part of 2026/27 revenue measures; the Finance Committee report (April 2026) has a minority report arguing pre-inspected Japanese imports are well maintained and that the cap would raise prices; the certificate of financial implication shows an expected revenue loss of UGX 19 billion in FY2026/27; whether it passed is unconfirmed [https://bills.parliament.ug/attachments/FPED3-26-Report%20on%20the%20Traffic%20and%20Road%20Safety%20(Amendment)%20Bill,%202026%20%20(Including%20Minority%20Report).pdf, Apr 2026 (search summary)].
- Environmental levy: the Finance Act 2006 imposes a levy on imported vehicles eight years or older; the 2018 amendment raised the exempt age from five to eight years; one source puts the levy at 50 percent of value for vehicles from five to 15 years and another describes it as applying to vehicles between nine and 15 years. CONFLICT on bands; check the current law [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/business/prosper/bumpy-ride-for-motorists-with-new-tax-on-used-cars-5414316, date not shown (search summary)].
- No maximum operating age for a taxi already on the road was found in any source. The Monitor in March 2023 said most Kampala taxis were up to 30 years old and gave no average [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/why-kampala-city-s-taxis-are-falling-apart-4152234, 9 Mar 2023].

Mandatory vehicle inspection
- SGS (Swiss) was contracted for mandatory inspections in 2015 with operations from 2016, Parliament halted the programme the next year over fees and public outcry, and a Cabinet decision ended the arrangement (KFM item March 2023) [https://kfm.co.ug/sgs-car-inspection-deal-terminated, date not shown, summary says Mar 2023].
- SGS compensation: one report says government agreed to pay Shs109bn with Shs30bn paid; another says SGS demanded more than Shs200bn. CONFLICT [https://independent.co.ug/govt-sgs-compensation-at-30-percent, date not shown].
- July 2025: the ministry began a mandatory automated inspection system starting with the government fleet [https://allafrica.com/stories/202507250242.html, Jul 2025 per URL code].
- 5 December 2025: the Ministry of Works and Transport took over nationwide inspection from SGS [https://eagle.co.ug/2025/12/05/govt-takes-over-vehicle-inspections-from-swiss-firm-sgs/, 5 Dec 2025 (summary)].
- 23 December 2025: ministry and police published reduced fees; initial coverage was government vehicles, public service vehicles on fixed routes, private omnibuses, tourist vehicles and police-referred DMC vehicles; private cars, taxis, trucks and motorcycles were to be phased in with dates to be announced [https://eagle.co.ug/2025/12/23/govt-unveils-reduced-motor-vehicle-inspection-fees-as-mandatory-checks-take-effect, 23 Dec 2025].
- Fees (Shs): motorcycles and tricycles 5,500; cars and dual-purpose 52,800; passenger vehicles with 8 to 20 passengers 49,500; 21 to 60 passengers 55,000; over 60 passengers 60,000. The article does not name 14-seater taxis; they would fall in the 8 to 20 band by seat count (my inference). Free re-inspection within 30 days of failure, 10 percent of fee after 30 days [https://eagle.co.ug/2025/12/23/govt-unveils-reduced-motor-vehicle-inspection-fees-as-mandatory-checks-take-effect, 23 Dec 2025].
- Centres: Namanve (Jinja Road), Kawanda (Bombo Road), Nabbingo (Masaka Road), Namulanda (Entebbe Road), plus Bugema, Mbale, Layibi, Gulu, Kora Norya, Mbarara. Test scope: brakes, suspension, lighting, exhaust emissions, general condition [https://eagle.co.ug/2025/12/23/govt-unveils-reduced-motor-vehicle-inspection-fees-as-mandatory-checks-take-effect, 23 Dec 2025].
- Commercial vehicles annually and private saloons and SUVs every two years (search summary) [https://eagle.co.ug/2025/12/05/govt-takes-over-vehicle-inspections-from-swiss-firm-sgs/, 5 Dec 2025].
- In the crackdown the same government facilities issue the "automated fitness test" pass and Certificate of Fitness [https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/only-69-impounded-taxis-cleared-to-return-to-kampala-roads-says-works-ministry-5618090, 3 Oct 2026].

PSV licensing and route charts (buses)
- 17 August 2026, announced by Minister Byamukama: all existing public service bus route charts are recalled; ownership is audited first; operators verified and new charts issued within one month; buses must pass a full inspection at Namanve before a new chart or licence; un-gazetted stages, roadside loading points and illegal bus parks closed; drivers vetted with one-year badges, a refresher course within 24 months and biometric log-in; a joint team of ministry, police, KCCA and local governments oversees it; no fines were specified. It followed a 16 July crash of a school bus in Kapchorwa that police said killed 24 [https://allafrica.com/stories/202608200167.html, 19 Aug 2026, originally Nile Post]. The article covers buses; whether the same recall applies to 14-seaters is not stated.

Automated fines
- The 2024 statutory instrument raised penalties (speeding up to Shs600,000), cut payment time from 28 days to 72 hours and added a 50 percent late surcharge; fines go to the registered owner [search-summary of https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/traffic-fines-throw-car-dealers-under-the-bus-5075486, 2025, exact date not shown].

## 4. Policy and law (Priority 4)

### 4.1 E-mobility policy and incentives

- National E-Mobility Strategy: aims to make Uganda a net source of e-mobility solutions, local production capacity of half a million vehicles by 2030 with 65 percent local content, and electrification of public transport such as buses, motorcycles and light rail. Launch year is disputed (2023 per AllAfrica, 2024 per Techpoint) [https://allafrica.com/stories/202511060049.html, 6 Nov 2025 per URL code; https://techpoint.africa/news/uganda-electric-motorcycle-tax-breaks/, date not shown]. The strategy document is hosted at [https://sti.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/National-E-Mobility-Strategy.pdf, Jul 2024 per URL] (not opened).
- A secondary summary says it targets public transport and motorcycles electrified by 2030, passenger vehicles by 2040, about USD 1.74 billion over five years, and 3,500 charging stations and 10,000 fast chargers by 2040, with only two stations in Kampala then. This is a blog-style source and unverified [https://nativemedia.odoo.com/zh_CN/blog/发现非洲-2/ugandas-e-mobility-report-2024-a-glimpse-into-africas-electric-future-86, 2024].
- Incentives in the strategy: ten-year income tax exemption for makers of EVs, batteries, charging equipment and bodies, and VAT exemptions for locally made EVs and charging equipment and services [https://plusnews.ug/?p=31414, date not shown].
- The 2024/25 budget exempted e-vehicles from 18 percent VAT, stamp duty and income tax [https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business/uganda-banks-on-tax-exemption-incentive-for-e-mobility-drive--4658852, Jun 2024 per summary]; Techpoint says an earlier import-duty waiver was reversed within months and a fully built import carries 25 percent duty [https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business/uganda-reinstates-tax-on-e-vehicles-4673578, 2024, exact date not shown; https://techpoint.africa/news/uganda-electric-motorcycle-tax-breaks/, date not shown].
- In 2026 income tax was removed for local e-motorcycle assemblers during their first five years [https://techpoint.africa/news/uganda-electric-motorcycle-tax-breaks/, 2026, exact date not shown].
- Electricity: the Q2 2026 domestic tariff in one summary is Shs250 per kWh for the first 15 units and Shs756.2 for 16 to 80 units; I could not tie this to a single page. No ERA tariff specific to EV charging was found, and nothing on UEDCL after the Umeme concession ended. For comparison, Kenya's e-mobility tariff is Sh16 peak and Sh8 off-peak per kWh [https://nation.africa/kenya/business/state-mulls-cheaper-power-for-electric-cars-charging-4646166, date not shown; ERA search results at https://observer.ug/?p=56043 and https://ugbusiness.com/?p=18933, dates not shown].

- NDP IV and Vision 2040 transport goals: not retrieved (see Gaps).
- GKMA Urban Development Programme: House cleared a World Bank loan headlined US$556 million; a search summary breaks it into US$518 million loan and US$48 million grant (sum 566; CONFLICT with headline) plus an AFD loan of US$42.66 million; it is mainly roads, drainage and markets [https://www.parliament.go.ug/news/6887/house-clears-us556-million-world-bank-loan-greater-kampala, date not shown (summary)]. ### 4.3 Licensing, traffic law and PPP

- PSV licensing: see Section 1.7 (August 2026 bus rules; inspection before route chart). Route-chart system dates from 2020 (Section 2).
- Traffic and Road Safety Act: 2018 amendment (age 15 import ban); 2026 amendment bill (age 13) (Section 1.7).
- PPP Act 2015 process: register project with the Ministry of Finance PPP unit; feasibility and structure; competitive bidding; major PPPs need cabinet and parliamentary approval. No 2026 official statement on privatising or franchising taxi routes was found.

### 4.4 Data protection, CCTV and trip data

- The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 regulates collection and processing of personal data, applies to controllers inside Uganda and those handling Ugandans' data abroad, and creates the Personal Data Protection Office under NITA-U; controllers must register and renew annually [https://ulii.org/en/akn/ug/act/2019/9/eng@2019-05-03, 2019; https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/08/uganda-data-protection-regulator-clarifies-compliance-requirements-for-offshore-entities, Aug 2025].
- PDPO decisions: in July 2025 against Google it said prior permission for each cross-border transfer is not required but records of legal basis and safeguards must be kept [https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/08/uganda-data-protection-regulator-clarifies-compliance-requirements-for-offshore-entities, Aug 2025].
- Vehicle tracking: Human Rights Watch criticised the Intelligent Transport Monitoring System (from 1 November 2023; cameras plus mandatory tracking devices) as a privacy risk and urged government to scrap it [https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/11/14/uganda-rights-concerns-over-license-plate-tracking, 14 Nov 2023].
- A PDPO letter dated 11 September 2026 acknowledged concerns on retention, purpose limitation, cross-border transfer and security in Uber's exit and said regulatory action had begun [https://monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/does-uber-s-exit-put-personal-data-at-risk--5626822, Sep 2026 per summary].
- No source tying the Act to CCTV in public vehicles or to taxi trip data was found.
